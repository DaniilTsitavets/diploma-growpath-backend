## Growing lavender from seed

Lavender seeds are slow. Germination takes 2 to 4 weeks and sometimes up to three months. Keeping the seeds in the fridge for 4 to 6 weeks before sowing improves the result. Press them onto the surface of gritty compost without covering them, because they need light, and keep them at about 18-21 °C and barely moist. Young plants are small for the first year.

## Sun and light

Lavender needs full sun, at least 6 hours a day, and does best in the brightest spot you have. Without enough light the plant becomes leggy and floppy with few flowers. Indoors, put it on a south-facing windowsill or outside on a sunny balcony during summer.

## Soil and drainage

Lavender needs very free-draining, gritty or sandy soil that is neutral to slightly alkaline, with a pH of 6.5 to 8. Mix potting compost with at least a third grit or perlite. Poor, lean soil suits it; rich soil gives soft growth with fewer flowers. Use a terracotta pot with drainage holes, and never leave water standing in the saucer.

## Watering lavender

Overwatering is the most common cause of death. Water a new plant regularly for the first season, then only when the soil is completely dry about 5 cm down. Lavender tolerates drought far better than wet roots. In winter water just enough to stop the pot drying out completely. Wilting and grey, yellowing shoots together with wet soil mean root rot.

## Feeding

Lavender needs little or no fertiliser. Rich feeding makes soft, leafy growth and fewer, less fragrant flowers. A light dose of low-nitrogen fertiliser in spring is enough for a plant in a pot.

## Pruning

Prune every year straight after flowering, in late summer. Cut back the flower stems and about 2 to 3 cm of the green growth below them so the plant stays compact. Never cut into the old, bare, woody stems, because they rarely shoot again. A plant that is woody and bare in the centre is past its best after 5 to 8 years and is best replaced with a new one.

## Harvesting lavender

Cut the flower stems when about a third of the buds on the spike have opened, in the morning after the dew has dried. Cut long stems and tie them in small bunches, then hang them upside down in a dry, airy and dark place for two to three weeks. Harvesting is a good moment to prune.

## Winter and cold

English lavender is quite hardy and survives frost down to about -15 °C when the soil drains well. French lavender, with the small rabbit-ear petals on top, is less hardy and tolerates only light frost, so keep it in a pot that can move into a bright, cool, frost-free place. Cold wet soil in winter does more harm than the frost itself.

## Pests and problems

Lavender has few pests. Spittlebugs leave white froth on the stems and can be washed off with water. Rosemary beetle chews the leaves and can be picked off by hand. Grey, rotting stems and a musty smell after a wet spell point to a fungal disease caused by poor drainage and humid air.
