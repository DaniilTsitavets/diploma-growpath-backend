## Growing basil from seed

Sow basil seeds about 5 mm deep in moist seed compost and keep them at 21-25 °C. Seeds usually germinate in 5 to 10 days. Keep the compost lightly moist, never wet, and give the seedlings plenty of light as soon as they sprout. Thin the seedlings to one strong plant every few centimetres, or move them into their own pots once they have two pairs of true leaves.

## Light

Basil needs 6 to 8 hours of direct sun a day. Indoors, a south-facing window works best; a grow lamp for 10 to 12 hours a day is a good substitute in winter. A plant that stretches towards the window with long gaps between the leaves is not getting enough light.

## Watering basil

Keep the soil evenly moist but never soggy. Water when the top 2 cm of soil feel dry, pour water at the base in the morning and let the excess drain away. Wilting leaves that perk up after watering mean the plant was too dry. Yellow lower leaves and a pot that stays wet for days point to overwatering and a risk of root rot, so always use a pot with drainage holes.

## Soil and pot

Use a light, well-drained potting mix with some compost and a pH between 6.0 and 7.0. A pot at least 20 cm wide gives the roots room; a single basil plant in a tiny pot dries out and stalls quickly. Do not let the pot stand in a saucer of water.

## Feeding

Feed basil every 2 to 3 weeks with a balanced liquid fertiliser diluted to half the strength on the label. Too much nitrogen gives big, pale and less aromatic leaves. Do not feed a dry or stressed plant; water it first.

## Pinching and pruning

Once the plant has about six leaves, pinch out the growing tip just above a pair of leaves. Two new shoots grow from that spot, so repeated pinching makes the plant bushy instead of tall and thin. Remove flower buds as soon as they appear, because flowering makes the leaves bitter and ends the harvest. Never remove more than a third of the plant at once.

## Harvesting basil

Pick basil in the morning, cutting a stem just above a pair of leaves so the plant can branch again. Use the leaves fresh. Do not keep basil in the fridge: leaves stored at low temperature turn dark and slimy. Keep cut stems in a glass of water at room temperature instead.

## Temperature and cold

Basil is a tender plant that grows best between 18 and 30 °C. Below about 10 °C the leaves develop black spots and the plant stops growing. Keep it away from cold draughts and cold window glass on winter nights, and move outdoor pots inside before the first cold nights.

## Pests and diseases

Aphids, whitefly and spider mites feed on the underside of the leaves; wash them off with water or spray with insecticidal soap. Downy mildew shows as yellow patches on the top of the leaf and grey fuzz underneath: remove affected leaves, water at the base instead of over the leaves and improve air circulation. Fusarium wilt makes whole stems collapse and cannot be cured, so discard the plant and do not reuse its soil.

## Yellow leaves and other problems

Yellow lower leaves usually mean overwatering, poor drainage or a lack of nitrogen. Brown, crisp leaf edges come from underwatering, dry air or fertiliser burn. A tall plant with a flower spike has started to bolt: cut the spike off and pinch the plant back to keep leaf production going.
