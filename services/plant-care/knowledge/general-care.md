## Watering basics

Check the soil before you water. Push a finger about 2 to 3 cm into the soil: if it feels dry, water; if it is still damp, wait. Water slowly until some drains from the holes, then empty the saucer after about 15 minutes. How often depends on the pot size, the season and the light, so a fixed schedule only works as a rough guide. A pot that feels much lighter than after the last watering is dry.

## Signs of overwatering and underwatering

An overwatered plant has yellow lower leaves, soft stems, soil that stays wet for days, sometimes a sour smell and black roots. An underwatered plant droops, the leaf edges turn brown and crisp, and the soil pulls away from the pot edge. If the plant droops although the soil is wet, suspect root rot, not thirst. Let the soil dry and repot into fresh mix if the roots are brown and mushy.

## Drainage and pots

Every pot needs drainage holes. Gravel at the bottom does not improve drainage; free-draining potting mix does. Terracotta dries faster than plastic, so plants in it need water more often. Do not leave the pot standing in water and lift it off the saucer after watering.

## Light basics

Most herbs and chili peppers need at least 6 hours of direct light. A south-facing window gives the most light in the northern hemisphere, east and west windows give less. A plant with long thin stems and pale leaves is reaching for light. A grow lamp placed 20 to 30 cm above the plant for 12 to 14 hours a day makes up for a dark room. Turn the pot a quarter every week so that growth stays even.

## Fertilising

Feed during the growing season with a balanced liquid fertiliser at the dilution on the label or weaker. Plants in pots use up nutrients and need feeding every 2 to 4 weeks. Do not feed a dry, sick or newly repotted plant. White crust on the soil is a sign of salt build-up: flush the pot with plenty of water and feed less.

## Repotting

Repot when roots grow out of the drainage holes or circle the inside of the pot, or when the soil dries out within a day. Choose a pot one size larger, 2 to 5 cm wider, with drainage holes. The best time is spring. Water the plant well a day before, loosen the root ball gently and fill around it with fresh potting mix.

## Common pests

Aphids, spider mites and whitefly live under the leaves. Isolate the plant, spray it with water and wipe the leaves, then use insecticidal soap every few days until the pests are gone. Webs and tiny speckles on the leaves mean spider mites, which like hot, dry air. Yellow sticky traps catch whitefly and other flying insects. Check new plants for pests before putting them next to the others.

## Fungus gnats and mould on the soil

Small black flies around the pot are fungus gnats; their larvae live in constantly wet soil. Let the top 3 to 5 cm of soil dry out between waterings, remove the top layer of soil and add a layer of sand or grit. Yellow sticky traps catch the adults. White mould on the soil surface is harmless to the plant but shows the soil stays too wet and the air is still, so water less and improve air movement.

## Humidity, temperature and air movement

Most herbs prefer moderate humidity, airy rooms and temperatures of 18 to 25 °C. Keep plants away from radiators, air conditioners and cold draughts. Stagnant, humid air encourages mildew and grey mould, while very dry air from heating brings spider mites. A small fan or an open window on a mild day keeps air moving.

## Yellow leaves, brown tips and drooping

Yellow lower leaves are usually caused by overwatering, poor drainage or a lack of nutrients; old leaves also yellow naturally. Brown leaf tips and edges come from underwatering, dry air, too much fertiliser or salts in the soil. A drooping plant with dry soil needs water, and one with wet soil needs less water and a check of the roots. Sudden leaf drop often follows a change of place, cold or heat stress.

## Hardening off and seedlings

Seedlings need bright light, steady moisture and warmth. Thin them early so each plant has space and air. Before moving young plants outside, harden them off: put them outdoors in a sheltered place for a few hours a day for 7 to 10 days, and increase the time slowly. Move them out only when nights stay above 10 °C.
