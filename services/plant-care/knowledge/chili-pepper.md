## Growing chili from seed

Chili seeds need warmth to sprout: 25-30 °C is ideal and they are very slow below 20 °C. Sow them 5 mm deep in moist seed compost and keep them on a heat mat or a warm spot. Seeds usually germinate in 7 to 14 days, sometimes up to three weeks. As soon as seedlings appear they need strong light, otherwise they become tall and weak. Move each seedling to its own small pot when it has two pairs of true leaves.

## Light and temperature

Chili plants need 6 to 8 hours of direct sun or a grow lamp for 12 to 14 hours a day. They grow best at 20-30 °C. Growth stops below about 15 °C, and a cold night can set the plant back for weeks, so keep it away from cold windows and draughts.

## Watering chili

Water thoroughly when the top 2 to 3 cm of soil are dry and empty the saucer afterwards. Chili plants dislike both soggy soil and long dry spells. Overwatering turns the lower leaves yellow and rots the roots, while very dry soil makes the leaves droop and curl. Steady, even moisture gives the best crop.

## Soil and pot

Use a rich, free-draining potting mix and a pot with drainage holes. Seedlings go into small pots and are moved up in steps to a final pot of 5 to 10 litres, about 25 to 30 cm wide. A pot that is too large for a small plant stays wet for too long.

## Feeding

Feed with a balanced fertiliser while the plant is growing leaves. When the first flowers open, switch to a potassium-rich tomato fertiliser every 1 to 2 weeks following the label. Too much nitrogen gives a big green plant with few flowers and little fruit.

## Flowers and fruit set

Flowers that drop off without making fruit are called blossom drop. The common causes are night temperatures below 15 °C or days above 32 °C, uneven watering, too little light and too much nitrogen. Keep conditions steady. Indoors there are no insects to pollinate the flowers, so shake the plant gently or transfer pollen from flower to flower with a soft brush.

## Harvesting chilies

Pick chilies when they have reached their full size and colour for the variety; the colour usually changes from green to red, yellow or orange. Cut the fruit with a short piece of stem using scissors. Wear gloves because capsaicin burns skin and eyes, and wash your hands afterwards. Picking regularly encourages the plant to produce more fruit.

## Pests and diseases

Aphids, spider mites, whitefly and fungus gnats are the usual pests; check the underside of the leaves and rinse them off or use insecticidal soap. A dark, sunken, leathery patch at the bottom of the fruit is blossom end rot, caused by uneven watering and poor calcium uptake; water evenly and remove affected fruit. Grey mould appears in cool, humid and stagnant air, so provide air movement.

## Overwintering

Chili plants are perennial in a warm place. In autumn cut the plant back by about half, move it to a bright spot at 10-15 °C and water only sparingly. In spring, when days lengthen, repot into fresh soil and start feeding again.
